## Confidence Adjustments

**Raised to documented.** The regulatory classification of OT-SIEM as a CDA was previously asserted without a pinned citation. RG 5.71 Revision 1 Section C.3.1.3 lists as criterion 5 of six digital assets that "protect any of the above from a cyberattack, up to and including the DBT." OT-SIEM meets that criterion directly, so the classification line at the top of each map is now documented rather than assumed. Revision 1 adds a sixth criterion covering balance-of-plant equipment, so the criterion is cited by number as 5 of 6 rather than simply 5.

**Raised to documented.** The three rows citing RG 5.71 Revision 1 for data diode limitations and portable media pathways are documented. An earlier pass of this verification recorded ML22258A204 as an unverifiable citation and downgraded these rows on that basis. That was wrong. RG 5.71 Revision 1 was issued February 2023 and published at 88 FR 9117 on February 13, 2023, and ML22258A204 is its correct ADAMS accession number. The document was retrieved and read. The supporting text sits in Section C.3.2, which states that use of a data diode alone does not provide adequate protection and that exploits associated with supply chain, portable media and mobile devices, wireless, and physical presence pathways can circumvent it. The rows now carry that section number rather than a bare accession number.

**Raised to documented, new citation.** RG 5.71 Revision 1 Section C.4.1 is the only place in the guide that names SIEM directly. It lists continuous monitoring of inbound and outbound network traffic and analysis of event logs, and automated collection and analysis of network traffic and CDA events and logs to provide real-time notification of security events and support incident response and forensic analysis, then states that these actions support anomaly detection by a HIDS, NIDS, or SIEM. This text does not appear in Revision 0, which is why it did not surface in the first pass. It now supports OT-SIEM's regulatory function description in the IT layer map and the alert output row in the physical layer map.

**Raised to documented.** The three architecture feed rows added to the OT layer map, RMS, PSI, and PPC, are documented against the CS 581 Plant Architecture Diagram, which is a course source rather than an inference. These are the best-supported rows in the OT map.

**Lowered to inferred.** The DNP3 parser row in Layer 2 was the strongest-rated row in that table and does not survive verification at that level. CISA ICSA-13-291-01B is a real advisory that says what the map claims it says, but its scope is master and slave station software, not monitoring sensors. The bridge from that scope to an OT-SIEM sensor's parser is inference and is now labeled as one.

**Lowered to inferred.** The SPAN mirror port row previously attributed a switch-CPU-overload mechanism to NIST Appendix E.2.2 and Section 5.2.3. Neither was confirmed to state that mechanism. The tap-as-monitoring-interface claim stays, the overload mechanism is now marked as not stated by the cited sources.

**Lowered to inferred.** The active scanning row previously stated as documented that NIST separates active from passive scanning because active scanning can disrupt fragile legacy OT devices. The separation of E.2.2 and E.2.3 is confirmed from the table of contents; the stated reason for the separation comes from body text that was not retrieved. The risk characterization is now inference from the fact of the separation.

**Unchanged, and confirmed appropriate.** The vendor cloud analytics egress row in the IT layer remains theoretical. Neither Source A nor Source B addresses cloud-connected OT monitoring platforms at licensed facilities, which is the correct basis for the lowest confidence tier rather than an oversight.

**Resolved.** An earlier pass listed as an open correction that the Layer 2 and Layer 3 tables still used a High, Medium, Low confidence scale. Both now use the course scale of documented, inferred, and theoretical. No action remains.

**Verified without change.** RG 5.71 Revision 1 Sections C.3.1.3 (Identification of Critical Digital Assets), C.3.1.4 (Review and Validation), C.3.2.1 (Defensive Architecture), C.3.3.2.5 (Physical Protection), and C.3.4 (Incorporating the Cybersecurity Program into the Physical Protection Program) all exist under those numbers with the content cited. On the NIST side, Sections 2.3.5, 5.2.3, 6.2.7, 6.2.10, 6.2.11, 6.2.12, and 6.3, and Appendix E subsections E.1.2 and E.2.1 through E.2.5, all carry the section numbers and titles cited.

## What This Verification Cannot Resolve

Two citation-level items remain open. First, the Revision 1 Glossary beginning at page 49 was not retrieved, so the definitions of "adverse impact" and "security monitoring network" are verified against Revision 0 only. Both are load-bearing: adverse impact carries the alert-suppression argument in the physical layer map, and security monitoring network carries the central-server row in the OT map. Second, the body text of NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E on pages 207 through 212 was not retrieved, so what E.2.2 and E.2.3 actually say about passive versus active scanning risk is unconfirmed, and two Layer 2 rows were downgraded to inferred for that reason.

Beyond citations, the substantive limits are unchanged. RG 5.71 Appendices B and C were not retrieved, so which specific boundary-device and monitoring controls a licensee must apply to an OT-SIEM collector, as opposed to the architectural rule in C.3.2.1, is still open. Neither source states whether the RMS feed uses Syslog or OPC-UA in a given deployment, what data the PSI feed carries, whether the log forwarder to ICT-SIEM is truly unidirectional, or whether the platform's active scanning feature is enabled, and all four would require the site's approved cybersecurity plan and the CDA walkdown records described in C.3.1.4. The vendor product identity, its patch level, and its sensor-to-manager protocol are outside both sources entirely and would need NDA architecture documentation. Verification against public regulatory guidance can confirm that a claim is consistent with the rule, which is what this pass did, but it cannot confirm that any facility's OT-SIEM is built the way the rule describes.

# Attack Surface Map — OT Layer (Levels 1–3 / Plant)
## CS 581 Workshop 1 | Sindi Banda | September 5, 2026

**System:** OT Security Information & Event Management (OT-SIEM)
**Purdue Zones covered:** Levels 1–3 — Field devices, Control, Supervisory
**Regulatory classification:** Critical Digital Asset (CDA) under 10 CFR 73.54, security monitoring function operating within the Levels 1–3 defensive architecture described in RG 5.71 Section C.3.2.1.

---

## OT Layer Attack Surface

| Component | Role | Threat Vectors | Notable Gaps | Confidence & Provenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Passive network taps / SPAN mirror ports on Level 1-2 control network switches | Duplicates Level 1-2 network traffic to OT-SIEM sensors without touching production traffic | SPAN port misconfiguration can overload a legacy control switch's CPU, and the tap becomes a chokepoint whose compromise gives full read visibility into safety-related process traffic without touching a single PLC | Cannot confirm exact tap locations chosen by a licensee, or the specific switches configured for mirroring | Documented: NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2.2 (Passive Scanning); NIST SP 800-82r3 Section 5.2.3 (Layer 3, Network Security) |
| Protocol decode / deep packet inspection engine parsing Modbus, DNP3, and OPC-UA traffic | Parses captured traffic to identify assets, commands, and anomalies | ICS-CERT's DNP3 implementation research found improper input validation across numerous vendor DNP3 stacks, including third-party components embedded in other software. A monitoring sensor's own protocol parser is exactly this kind of embedded third-party stack | Cannot confirm the specific parsing library or software version running in a deployed sensor, or whether it has been patched against known implementation flaws | Documented: CISA ICSA-13-291-01B (DNP3 Implementation Vulnerability); NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2 (Network Monitoring, SIEM) |
| Host-based log forwarding agent on Level 2 HMI and engineering workstations | Collects Windows event logs or syslog output from HMI and engineering workstations for correlation | The agent needs elevated log-read access or a listening port on the HMI. If the channel is not strictly one-way, a compromised OT-SIEM collector could push configuration back into an operator workstation | Cannot confirm whether nuclear HMIs specifically run a forwarding agent, or which authentication method that agent uses | Inferred: NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2.1 (Centralized Logging) describes the capability; no source confirms nuclear-specific HMI deployment |
| OT-SIEM central correlation and management server at Level 3 | Aggregates input from every Level 1-2 sensor, applies correlation rules, and generates alerts | This single node aggregates visibility across the whole site. A security monitoring platform is required to hold a security level equal to or greater than what it watches, meaning compromise of this node is functionally equivalent to compromising the highest-value CDA it monitors | Cannot confirm the vendor product, physical location, or redundancy configuration of the central server at a specific facility | Documented: NRC RG 5.71 Glossary (security monitoring network definition); NEI 08-09 Rev 6 Appendix E Section 6 (Security CDAs air-gapped or behind a unidirectional deterministic boundary device) |
| Passive sensor placement inside the Level 1 field controller network (RPS/EDGC segment) | Extends visibility into safety-system network traffic for detection purposes | RG 5.71 treats digital isolation of CDAs as the most secure option and states it is preferred whenever feasible. Installing even a passive tap on a Level 1 safety segment is a new connection point, and a compromised tap gives reconnaissance visibility into RPS communication timing and structure | Cannot confirm whether any licensee has actually placed a sensor inside a Level 1 safety segment, or whether digital isolation is maintained instead | Documented: NRC RG 5.71 Section C.3.2.1 (digital isolation preference) |
| Threat intelligence feed ingestion into the OT-SIEM correlation engine | Enriches correlation rules with external indicators of compromise and known adversary TTPs | Both cited sources prohibit a lower security level from initiating communication toward a higher one. A threat feed implemented as an outbound pull from Level 3 violates that rule directly and creates the exact egress channel the defensive architecture is designed to prevent | Cannot confirm whether a specific facility's threat intel ingestion is implemented as a compliant one-way push or as a rule-violating pull | Documented: NRC RG 5.71 Section C.3.2.1 (initiation of communications prohibition); NEI 08-09 Rev 6 Appendix E Section 6 (restriction on communication from lower to higher security levels) |
| Incident ticket generation and ITSM integration when a correlation rule fires | Automatically opens a tracked incident record when a correlation rule fires | The same initiation-direction problem as the threat intel feed. A direct outbound call from OT-SIEM to a Level 4 or cloud-hosted helpdesk platform is a Level 3-initiated path toward a higher level | Cannot confirm whether ITSM integration exists at all in a given deployment, or how it is architected if it does | Inferred: derived from NRC RG 5.71 Section C.3.2.1 applied to a common SIEM-to-ITSM pattern that neither RG 5.71 nor NIST SP 800-82r3 names directly |
| Active scanning capability for asset discovery, if enabled | Actively probes Level 1-2 devices to build a more complete asset inventory, where enabled | NIST separates active from passive scanning specifically because active scanning can disrupt fragile legacy OT devices. Enabling this feature turns OT-SIEM from a passive observer into a tool capable of causing an availability incident | Cannot confirm whether active scanning is enabled or disabled at any specific facility | Documented: NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2.2 (Passive Scanning) and E.2.3 (Active Scanning) |
| Portable media and field technician laptop interface for sensor configuration | Lets field technicians configure sensors and load firmware updates on site | RG 5.71's own discussion of data diode limitations names portable media as a pathway that circumvents boundary protections regardless of network architecture | Cannot confirm the physical access control or logging in place around field technician laptop use | Documented: NRC RG 5.71 Revision 1 (ML22258A204), portable media and mobile device pathway discussion |
| Sensor-to-manager communication channel linking distributed collectors to the central server | Carries processed event data from each distributed collector back to the central Level 3 server | This east-west traffic is often held to a lower standard than the Level 3/4 boundary because it is treated as internal. A sensor compromised at one plant area could pivot through this channel to the central server, bypassing zone boundaries between separate process areas | Cannot confirm the actual protocol used between distributed sensors and the central server, which is vendor-proprietary and rarely published | Inferred: derived from the centralized-SIEM architecture in NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2; no source specifies the protocol for a nuclear deployment |
| Behavioral anomaly detection baseline drawing on live process values | Learns normal process value ranges over time so it can flag deviations later | An adversary aware of the baseline period, often during commissioning or shortly after a plant modification, could introduce small anomalies gradually so they are learned as normal | Cannot confirm the actual baseline training window at a given facility, or whether it has ever been reviewed for tampering | Theoretical: NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2.5 (Behavioral Anomaly Detection) names the capability; the poisoning technique is extrapolated from general ICS anomaly-detection literature |

---

## Summary

The dominant OT-layer risk is that OT-SIEM's own sensing infrastructure, passive taps, protocol parsers, and physical placement inside Level 1-2 networks, becomes a new attack surface and a new physical intervention point precisely because it exists to add visibility that a fully air-gapped architecture would not otherwise require. Confidence is highest where NRC and NIST guidance state an explicit rule, such as the digital isolation preference or the initiation-direction prohibition, and drops sharply for anything describing how a specific facility has actually configured its sensors, since that detail sits in CDA walkdown records this analysis cannot access.

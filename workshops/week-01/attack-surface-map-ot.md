# Attack Surface Map — OT Layer (Levels 1–3 / Plant)
## CS 581 Workshop 1 | Sindi Banda | September 5, 2026

**System:** OT Security Information & Event Management (OT-SIEM)

**Purdue zones covered:** Levels 1 to 3, field devices, control, and supervisory

**System's own Purdue zone:** Level 3, Site Operations

**Regulatory classification:** Critical Digital Asset (CDA) under 10 CFR 73.54, meeting CDA identification criterion 5 of the six listed in RG 5.71 Rev. 1 Section C.3.1.3, operating within the defensive architecture described in RG 5.71 Rev. 1 Section C.3.2.1.

**Note on RG 5.71 level numbering:** RG 5.71 uses a five-level defensive architecture in which Level 4 is the innermost and most secure. This is inverted relative to the Purdue numbering in this map's headings. Claims sourced to RG 5.71 are stated in RG 5.71 terms.

**Note on architecture scope:** The first three rows are the inbound connections the CS 581 Plant Architecture Diagram actually shows for OT-SIEM. Rows marked with an asterisk are generic OT-SIEM capabilities described in NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E that the CS 581 architecture does not show for this plant. They are included as potential interfaces, not confirmed ones.

## OT Layer Attack Surface

| Component | Role | Threat Vectors | Notable Gaps | Confidence & Provenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RMS to OT-SIEM event feed | Carries radiation monitoring events from RMS at Level 3 into OT-SIEM. This is the only OT-SIEM feed the CS 581 architecture labels with a protocol, shown as Syslog / OPC-UA | Syslog carries no authentication of its own unless the transport provides it, so a feed that is not wrapped in TLS can be spoofed or suppressed by anything with access to that Level 3 segment. Because RMS readings drive emergency action level classification, false or missing RMS events in OT-SIEM degrade detection of exactly the condition the plant most needs to see | Cannot confirm whether the deployed feed uses Syslog or OPC-UA, or whether the transport is authenticated and encrypted | Documented: CS 581 Plant Architecture Diagram (RMS to OT-SIEM, Syslog / OPC-UA); NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2.1 (Centralized Logging) |
| PSI to OT-SIEM security event feed | Carries physical security events from PSI into OT-SIEM, which is where physical and cyber indicators are brought together | This feed is what makes correlated cyber-plus-physical detection possible. Suppressing or delaying it separates the two halves of an attack so that neither alone crosses an alert threshold. RG 5.71 requires the licensee to analyze, identify, and document physical security and cybersecurity interdependencies, and this feed is that interdependency in concrete form | Cannot confirm the protocol, the event types forwarded, or whether the feed is one-way | Documented: CS 581 Plant Architecture Diagram (PSI to OT-SIEM, security events); NRC RG 5.71 Rev. 1 Section C.3.4 (Incorporating the Cybersecurity Program into the Physical Protection Program) |
| PPC to OT-SIEM historian event feed | Carries historian events from the plant process computer into OT-SIEM. The architecture shows PPC bridging Level 3 and Level 2, and PPC is OT-SIEM's only path to DCS process data, since the diagram shows no direct DCS to OT-SIEM edge | All process visibility OT-SIEM has arrives through one system. Compromise or manipulation of PPC removes or falsifies OT-SIEM's entire view of control-layer activity in a single step, and the loss would not be visible in the RMS or PSI feeds. PPC straddling two security levels also means this feed crosses a level boundary inside the plant, not only at the corporate edge | Cannot confirm the protocol, the historian event set forwarded, or how the PPC level-straddle is implemented in boundary terms | Documented: CS 581 Plant Architecture Diagram (PPC to OT-SIEM, historian events; DCS to PPC, OPC-UA historian; no direct DCS to OT-SIEM edge); NRC RG 5.71 Rev. 1 Section C.3.2.1 (data flow between levels passes through a device that enforces security policy) |
| Passive network taps / SPAN mirror ports on Level 1-2 control network switches * | Duplicates Level 1-2 network traffic to OT-SIEM sensors without touching production traffic | A tap is a chokepoint whose compromise gives full read visibility into safety-related process traffic without touching a single PLC. SPAN port misconfiguration can also load a legacy control switch beyond its capacity, though no source read for this map states that mechanism directly | Cannot confirm exact tap locations chosen by a licensee, or the specific switches configured for mirroring | Inferred: NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2.2 (Passive Scanning) documents passive monitoring as a capability but does not state the switch-loading mechanism; the CS 581 architecture does not show taps for this plant |
| Protocol decode / deep packet inspection engine parsing Modbus, DNP3, and OPC-UA traffic * | Parses captured traffic to identify assets, commands, and anomalies | ICS-CERT's DNP3 implementation research found improper input validation across numerous vendor DNP3 stacks, including third-party components embedded inside other software packages. If a monitoring sensor's parser is one of those embedded stacks, a malformed packet crafted by an attacker already on the OT segment could target the sensor rather than a field device | Cannot confirm the specific parsing library or software version running in a deployed sensor, or whether it has been patched against known implementation flaws | Inferred: CISA ICSA-13-291-01B (DNP3 Implementation Vulnerability) documents the flaws in master and slave station software, not in passive monitoring sensors. Applying it to an OT-SIEM parser is inference from the advisory's note about embedded third-party components |
| Host-based log forwarding agent on Level 2 HMI and engineering workstations * | Collects Windows event logs or syslog output from HMI and engineering workstations for correlation | The agent needs elevated log-read access or a listening port on the HMI. If the channel is not strictly one-way, a compromised OT-SIEM collector could push configuration back into an operator workstation | Cannot confirm whether nuclear HMIs specifically run a forwarding agent, or which authentication method that agent uses | Inferred: NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2.1 (Centralized Logging) describes the capability; no source confirms nuclear-specific HMI deployment |
| OT-SIEM central correlation and management server | Aggregates input from every feed, applies correlation rules, and generates alerts | RG 5.71 requires a security monitoring network to be protected at a level equal to or greater than the levels it supports, which is why this node is treated as a high-consequence asset rather than a supporting one. It concentrates the plant's entire security detection capability in a single system, so its loss removes detection everywhere at once | Cannot confirm the vendor product, physical location, or redundancy configuration of the central server at a specific facility | Documented: NRC RG 5.71 Rev. 1 Section C.3.1.3 (CDA criterion 5, assets that protect others from cyberattack); NRC RG 5.71 Rev. 0 Glossary (security monitoring network, equal or greater security level). The Rev. 1 Glossary was not retrieved, so confirm the definition survived into Rev. 1 |
| Passive sensor placement inside the Level 1 field controller network (RPS/EDGC segment) * | Extends visibility into safety-system network traffic for detection purposes | RG 5.71 describes digital isolation of CDAs, meaning no digital communication pathways between a CDA and any other digital asset, as a mechanism for meeting many 10 CFR 73.54 requirements. Installing even a passive tap on a Level 1 safety segment adds a connection point that isolation would otherwise avoid, and a compromised tap gives reconnaissance visibility into RPS communication timing and structure | Cannot confirm whether any licensee has actually placed a sensor inside a Level 1 safety segment, or whether digital isolation is maintained instead | Documented: NRC RG 5.71 Rev. 1 Section C.3.2.1 (digital isolation as a mechanism for meeting 73.54 requirements). The CS 581 architecture shows RPS reaching DCS through a data diode carrying status only, and shows no OT-SIEM sensor inside the RPS segment |
| Threat intelligence feed ingestion into the OT-SIEM correlation engine * | Enriches correlation rules with external indicators of compromise and known adversary TTPs | An outbound request from OT-SIEM is the permitted direction, but the returning feed data travels inward from a lower security level to a higher one. RG 5.71 requires that kind of initiation to be handled on a deny-all, permit-by-exception basis with a complete justification and security risk analysis, so an ingestion path configured by vendor default rather than by analysis is the finding | Cannot confirm whether a specific facility ingests external threat intelligence at all, or how the return path is architected | Documented: NRC RG 5.71 Rev. 1 Section C.3.2.1 (deny-all, permit-by-exception for initiation from lower to higher levels). The CS 581 architecture does not show a threat intelligence feed |
| Incident ticket generation and ITSM integration when a correlation rule fires * | Automatically opens a tracked incident record when a correlation rule fires | The same direction problem as the threat intelligence feed. The outbound call is permitted, the acknowledgment or ticket status returning inward is not, unless it is an analyzed exception. A queue written locally and polled from the lower level avoids the return leg entirely | Cannot confirm whether ITSM integration exists at all in a given deployment, or how it is architected if it does | Inferred: derived from NRC RG 5.71 Rev. 1 Section C.3.2.1 applied to a common SIEM-to-ITSM pattern that neither RG 5.71 nor NIST SP 800-82r3 names directly |
| Active scanning capability for asset discovery, if enabled * | Actively probes Level 1-2 devices to build a more complete asset inventory, where enabled | NIST separates active from passive scanning as distinct capabilities. Enabling active scanning turns OT-SIEM from a passive observer into a system that transmits onto the control network, which changes its risk profile from confidentiality of monitored data to availability of monitored devices | Cannot confirm whether active scanning is enabled or disabled at any specific facility | Inferred: NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2.2 (Passive Scanning) and E.2.3 (Active Scanning) document both as separate capabilities. The body text distinguishing their OT risk was not retrieved, so the risk characterization is inference from the fact of the separation |
| Portable media and field technician laptop interface for sensor configuration * | Lets field technicians configure sensors and load firmware updates on site | RG 5.71 names portable media and mobile device pathways among the exploits that can circumvent the protection a data diode provides, and separately treats sneaker nets, in which data or software is carried manually on transportable storage media, as an indirect connection to a CDA. Either way, a technician's laptop bypasses every network-layer control in this map | Cannot confirm the physical access control or logging in place around field technician laptop use | Documented: NRC RG 5.71 Rev. 1 Section C.3.2 (portable media and mobile device pathways circumventing a data diode); NRC RG 5.71 Rev. 1 Section C.3.1.3 (sneaker nets as indirect CDA connections) |
| Sensor-to-manager communication channel linking distributed collectors to the central server * | Carries processed event data from each distributed collector back to the central server | RG 5.71 states that a pathway is in scope whether it runs between higher and lower security levels, between assets on the same level, or within a device such as a switch or multiplexer. That makes east-west collector traffic in scope even though it never crosses a level boundary, and a sensor compromised in one plant area could pivot through it to the central server | Cannot confirm the actual protocol used between distributed sensors and the central server, which is vendor-proprietary and rarely published | Inferred: NRC RG 5.71 Rev. 1 Section C.3.1.3 documents the same-level pathway scope; the distributed collector architecture itself is inferred from NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2 and is not shown in the CS 581 architecture |
| Behavioral anomaly detection baseline drawing on live process values * | Learns normal process value ranges over time so it can flag deviations later | An adversary aware of the baseline period, often during commissioning or shortly after a plant modification, could introduce small anomalies gradually so they are learned as normal | Cannot confirm the actual baseline training window at a given facility, or whether it has ever been reviewed for tampering | Theoretical: NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2.5 (Behavioral Anomaly Detection) names the capability; the poisoning technique is extrapolated from general ICS anomaly-detection literature and is not documented in any nuclear-specific source |

## Summary

The dominant OT-layer risk is concentration. The CS 581 architecture gives OT-SIEM three inbound feeds and no direct connection to the control network, which means all of its process visibility arrives through PPC, all of its physical security visibility arrives through PSI, and all of its radiological visibility arrives through RMS. Each feed is a single point whose loss is silent, and the central correlation server is a single point whose loss removes detection plant-wide. Where a deployment adds the generic capabilities in the asterisked rows, taps, agents, external feeds, and active scanning, it buys visibility by adding connection points that digital isolation would otherwise avoid, so each one has to be justified rather than assumed. Confidence is highest for the three architecture feeds and for claims where RG 5.71 Rev. 1 states an explicit rule, and drops sharply for anything describing how a specific facility has configured sensors it may not have.

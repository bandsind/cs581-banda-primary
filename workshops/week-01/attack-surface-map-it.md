# Attack Surface Map — IT Layer (Level 4 / Corporate)
## CS 581 Workshop 1 | Sindi Banda | September 5, 2026

**System:** OT Security Information & Event Management (OT-SIEM)
**Purdue Zone:** Level 4 — Corporate / Enterprise Network
**Regulatory classification:** Critical Digital Asset (CDA) under 10 CFR 73.54. OT-SIEM performs a security monitoring function that protects SSEP-supporting systems from cyber attack, which meets the RG 5.71 CDA identification criterion covering assets that protect a critical system from cyber attack up to and including the design basis threat.

---

## IT Layer Attack Surface

| Component | Role | Threat Vectors | Notable Gaps | Confidence & Provenance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OT-SIEM to corporate SOC and ICT-SIEM log/alert forwarder | Exports correlated OT security events and alerts from the Level 3 SIEM to the corporate SOC and ICT-SIEM for enterprise-wide visibility and reporting | The forwarder is the boundary itself. If implemented as a two-way management channel instead of a one-way export, it violates the mandated Level 3 to Level 4 flow direction and gives a corporate-side actor a path back into the OT-SIEM | Cannot confirm whether the forwarder is truly unidirectional in a given facility's implementation or supports a query-back channel for remote diagnostics | Documented: NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2.1 (Centralized Logging); NRC RG 5.71 Section C.3.2.1 (one-way data flow requirement) |
| Data diode / unidirectional gateway at the Level 3/4 boundary | Enforces the mandated one-way data flow at the Level 3/4 boundary so no session can be initiated from Level 4 into Level 3 | A data diode alone does not satisfy defense-in-depth. Supply chain, portable media, wireless, and physical-presence pathways can bypass it, and it is a single physical chokepoint whose failure or misconfiguration removes the boundary entirely | Cannot confirm whether the diode is a single device or a redundant pair, its fail-safe behavior, or its exact physical location relative to the CDA boundary | Documented: NRC RG 5.71 Section C.3.2.1; NRC RG 5.71 Revision 1 (ML22258A204), data diode limitations |
| Vendor remote support and diagnostic access to the OT-SIEM management interface | Lets the OT-SIEM vendor tune correlation rules, apply patches, and troubleshoot the platform without a technician on site | Vendor access for rule tuning and troubleshooting is a standing remote pathway from the corporate network. Without MFA and session recording, it is a direct route to the SIEM's own configuration | Cannot confirm whether MFA, session recording, and time-limited access are actually enforced on this path, or whether a shared or standing credential is used | Documented: NIST SP 800-82r3 Section 6.2.10 (Remote Access); NRC RG 5.71 Section C.3.1.4 (review and validation of connectivity pathways) |
| Vendor software update/patch channel for the SIEM platform | Delivers signed software and detection-rule updates to keep the OT-SIEM platform's logic current | The update mechanism of a monitoring platform is itself a supply chain target. SolarWinds Orion, a network monitoring platform with a comparable centralized visibility role, was compromised at the build stage and used to gain footholds across federal and critical infrastructure networks through a routine update | Cannot confirm the specific SIEM product deployed at a given facility, its current patch level, or its update-signing verification process | Documented: CISA Activity Alert AA20-352A / Emergency Directive 21-01 (SolarWinds Orion supply chain compromise); NIST SP 800-82r3 Section 6.2.11 (Flaw Remediation and Patch Management) |
| Shared time synchronization (NTP) source | Synchronizes OT-SIEM's internal clock with a reference time source so correlated events across sensors carry consistent, orderable timestamps | If the OT-SIEM's time source traces through a corporate-facing NTP server rather than a dedicated Level 2/3 source, that low-bandwidth service is a persistent path across the boundary | Cannot confirm the actual NTP source hierarchy in use at any specific facility | Inferred: NIST SP 800-82r3 Section 6.2.12 (Time Synchronization) names the control area; no source confirms a specific facility's architecture |
| Shared identity and authentication (AD/LDAP/SSO) for SOC analyst logins | Authenticates SOC analysts and administrators who log into the OT-SIEM console | Where the same analysts monitor both IT and OT alerts, OT-SIEM logins sometimes ride the corporate identity provider instead of a separate OT credential store. A compromised corporate account would then reach the OT-SIEM console directly | Cannot confirm whether OT-SIEM login credentials are separated from corporate Active Directory at a specific site | Inferred: NIST SP 800-82r3 defense-in-depth list names separate authentication mechanisms and credentials for OT versus corporate users as the recommended practice, not a confirmed configuration |
| Web-based SIEM analyst console/dashboard | Presents correlated alerts, dashboards, and investigation tools to analysts through a browser | If the console is hosted in or routed through the Level 3/4 DMZ rather than confined to Level 3, it becomes a browser-based attack surface (credential theft, session hijacking, unpatched web framework flaws) reachable from the wider corporate network | Cannot confirm the console's actual network placement relative to the DMZ, or whether it is reachable from general corporate workstations | Inferred: NIST SP 800-82r3 Appendix E.2 (Network Monitoring, SIEM) documents the console's existence, not its placement |
| Vendor cloud analytics/threat intelligence telemetry egress | Sends anonymized telemetry to the vendor's cloud service for cross-customer threat correlation and enrichment, where the feature is enabled | An outbound telemetry channel from OT-SIEM through the corporate network to a vendor cloud service falls outside the inbound-only model RG 5.71 describes | Cannot confirm whether any licensed facility has this feature enabled at all; it may not exist in this environment | Theoretical: extrapolated from general commercial OT security platform architecture (industry vendor comparisons, 2026); no NRC or NIST source addresses cloud egress |

---

## Summary

The dominant IT-layer risk for OT-SIEM is that every path required for its stated function, log export to the corporate SOC, vendor patching, and threat intel enrichment, is also a path that could violate RG 5.71's mandatory one-way, higher-initiates architecture if it is implemented as an ordinary two-way integration instead of a purpose-built one-way one. The primary source of uncertainty is that public regulatory and technical guidance describes what the architecture must do, not how any specific licensee's OT-SIEM deployment is actually wired, so most rows above rest on inference from the rule rather than confirmation of an implementation.
